Rules & Safety

Jacana River Lodge and Mjejane Game Reserve Rules and Regulations

Please take a minute to familiarise yourself and your accompanying family, dependants and guests with these rules and regulations.

Rules & Safety

Welcome to Jacana River Lodge

Your enjoyment and well-being as a visitor to Jacana River Lodge and Mjejane Game Reserve is of utmost importance to our directors, shareholders and staff. Please take a minute of your time to familiarise yourself and your accompanying family, dependants and guests with these Rules and Regulations.

You are required to sign the Jacana River Lodge Indemnity on your arrival. By signing the Indemnity, you will be deemed to have read and understood all these rules and regulations. All directors, shareholders, their guests and visitors are obliged to sign the Indemnity. No-one will be allowed on game drives or remain in the Reserve unless the indemnities have been signed.

General

Please always follow the instructions and requests of the Lodge Manager. (Your safety and comfort are our priority.)

Jacana River Lodge is situated in a high-density wildlife zone. Be constantly aware of the possibility of encountering a dangerous animal, especially at night. Lion, leopard, hippos, elephant and buffalo inhabit the riverine environment and from time to time come within close proximity to Jacana River Lodge. A low electric fence separates the property from the free-roaming Big Five territory. Sometimes this fence is not live. Animals can jump over the fence or break through it, so do take extra care when animals are near the fence or lodge.

More about General

Take extra care to supervise the movement of children around the exterior precinct of Jacana River Lodge. Be observant at all times.

Persons who are under the influence of alcohol or other substances having a narcotic effect may not under any circumstances participate in any activities on Mjejane Game Reserve.

All persons under the age of 16 years who wish to participate in activities on the Reserve are to be accompanied and supervised at all times by an adult or their lawful guardian.

Do not walk barefoot, particularly at night, anywhere in the immediate area outside of Jacana River Lodge, for example when walking to rooms. It is advisable to wear closed shoes after dark and make use of a hands-free headlamp.

(Scorpions, spiders, centipedes and snakes may be encountered. The most dangerous snakes which could be encountered in the precinct of JRL include the Black Mamba, Snouted (Egyptian) Cobra, Mozambique Spitting Cobra, Boomslang and Puff Adder.)

The feeding of birds and animals is strictly prohibited in terms of the Reserve Rules.

Do not leave any food, sweets or cold drinks unattended on the verandas or in the deck area, boma, or in unlocked dustbins outside.

Monkeys

Mjejane Private Nature Reserve has troops of Vervet Monkeys. They patrol the area around Jacana River Lodge, and they see the lodges as an easy source of food.

Do not feed the monkeys and do not leave food, drinks or litter unattended. The consequences of this will be perpetual raiding, potential for injury to occupants and costly cleaning of Jacana River Lodge and replacement of damaged items. Everyone must be aware and disciplined in this regard to avoid these animals being put down.

Keep all windows and doors closed when you are not in the room, in order to prevent wild animals from entering.

It is advisable to keep the insect screens on the sliding doors closed at all times to keep snakes and scorpions out. It is not advisable to sleep with the doors open. You are advised to wear appropriate clothing and use repellents for mosquitoes, particularly during the rainy season.

You are advised to take adequate precautions against the sun.

Do not attract unwarranted attention by openly leaving cash, expensive jewellery, cameras, binoculars and other valuables unattended or unsecured. Please make use of the safes provided.

In consideration for the neighbours and the wildlife, reduce noise levels after 22h00.

Lodge Rules

Smoking

Jacana River Lodge is required by law to comply with the Tobacco Products Control Amendment Act, 2010.

There is a strict non-smoking policy applicable inside Jacana River Lodge. If smoking outside around Jacana River Lodge, please use ashtrays and ensure your cigarettes, etc. are properly extinguished. Please don’t leave cigarette butts in the bushveld.

Energy Conservation

There is limited electrical supply capacity in the Reserve.

  • Turn off your air-conditioning units when not in use. Try to limit the use of aircons.
  • Please switch off lights and appliances when not in use. (Conservative use of lighting in Jacana River Lodge creates a wonderful ambience at night.)

Water Conservation
  • Please keep your water usage within reason.
  • Please ensure that taps are properly turned off.
  • Where possible, re-use your room towels.

Fauna and Flora

Do not in any way disturb the plants, trees, insects and animals around Jacana River Lodge and anywhere on the Reserve. Please pick up any litter around Jacana River Lodge.

Use of Mjejane Game Reserve Roads and Speed Limits

Visitors to JRL are only allowed to drive in their private vehicles to JRL on the main access road that runs from the entrance gate through Mjejane Game Reserve to Erf 3, where Jacana River Lodge is located, or to the main Lodge and offices situated on Erf 1. Private vehicles must not deviate from the main access road, as this will constitute a material contravention of the Reserve Rules and will incur a fine. Ensure that your guests understand and adhere to this rule.

The maximum speed allowed on the Reserve main access road is 40km/h. There are signboards to this effect along the main access road. Please ensure that everyone is aware of this.

If you are driving in your own vehicle to the Kruger National Park access bridge, you must leave through the main Erf 3 gate and strictly keep to the clearly demarcated route. No deviations or short cuts will be tolerated. The bridge is temporarily closed.

Feedback and Problems

All problems must be taken up with the Lodge Manager, who is responsible for all aspects of Jacana River Lodge.

If there are any problems which cannot be resolved by the Lodge Manager, you are welcome to contact the Lodge Owner’s representative: Mariska Claassen, 066 293 7100 / info@jacanariverlodge.com

Open Boma Fires

Exercise extreme caution when making a fire in the boma. Only fires appropriate for cooking are allowed, i.e. no large fires or bonfires are allowed. Make sure you know where the nearest water source (hosepipe) is located. (Open boma fires are hazardous. It just requires one spark to ignite a blaze.)

  • Extinguish all open fires / braai coals in the braai and boma area prior to going to bed.
  • Do not leave open fires unattended. You must ensure that all coals are fully extinguished and that embers are under the ash cover provided before retiring from the boma.
  • Open fires are not permitted on windy nights.
  • You are not allowed to collect firewood from the precinct of Jacana River Lodge or anywhere in Mjejane Private Nature Reserve.
  • The thatched roof of Jacana River Lodge presents a great fire risk, so please exercise extreme caution.

Domestic Service

Our full-time domestic worker is available during the following hours:

  • Weekdays: 07:30 – 12:00 and 12:30 – 14:45
  • Weekends: 08:00 – 11:30
Please note: the domestic worker is unavailable on Sundays and Public Holidays.

The domestic worker travels to and from JRL on transport provided by the Reserve. This transport follows a fixed schedule so she can catch the return bus from the main gate on the N4. Please ensure she is not delayed.

Her primary duties are lodge-related and limited to cleaning rooms and verandas, making beds, washing and ironing towels, and dishwashing. Duties do not include packing away guests’ personal items, preparing food, babysitting, or washing/ironing personal clothing.

Game Viewing Vehicle

(Mjejane Game Reserve is a special concession area incorporated within and subject to the Rules and Regulations of the Kruger National Park and the National Parks Board.)

Your personal safety in the open game viewing vehicle is strongly dependent on you.

  • Listen to and follow the rules which your ranger will brief you on prior to game drives on the Reserve.
  • Do not participate in game drive viewing activities if you have pre-existing medical conditions, e.g. back problems, which could be aggravated by some rough driving conditions.
  • Your Ranger is not allowed to go off-road at any time and must comply with all speed limits.
  • There are strict rules of conduct and protocol at sightings with which your ranger is familiar. He is obliged to comply with them.
  • Speed limits must be obeyed.
  • Seat belts are provided. They are for compulsory use, as you will be driving on irregular road surfaces and angled departures.
  • Secure your valuables on the vehicle. They have a tendency to fall off the game viewing vehicles due to the irregular road surfaces.

Inventory

You will be provided, on arrival, with an Inventory list of the assets, equipment and usable items in the Lodge, which you will need to sign. Please spend a few minutes to check the Inventory and the existence of the items on the Inventory.

Any items missing or broken on the Inventory will be payable by you and will, as a first payment, be deducted from the deposit paid.

Mjejane Rules

A copy of the Rules issued by the Mjejane Administrative Office is set out below and forms part of this document.

  1. Unless in a designated area, visitors must remain inside their vehicle at all times. No part of your body may protrude from a window or sunroof or any other part of the vehicle. Doors must remain closed at all times.
  2. The maximum speed limit is 40km/h and vehicles may only drive on the main road. No diversions off the main road are permitted.
  3. Drivers of vehicles on the reserve must be in possession of a valid driver’s licence.
  4. The feeding or disturbance of animals is a serious offence. No plants, rocks, or animal material may be disturbed or removed.
  5. Visitors may not walk along the camp fence or pass the front of lodges; visitors are advised to stay on the designated roads or walkways. Visitors may not walk around the camp after dark.
  6. Cycling is prohibited within the camps; visitors are only allowed to cycle within the designated area between the N4 gate and the railway bridge.
  7. All wild animals must be treated with caution and respect; approaching and petting wild animals is not permitted and unsafe.
  8. Mjejane is a malaria zone. We advise that all visitors adhere to a medical doctor’s instructions.
  9. For any emergencies or incident reports, please contact the Reserve phone at 083 309 7087.
  10. Music/radios may not be played in such a manner that causes a disturbance to other guests and/or wild animals.
  11. Visitors are not allowed to write, mark or affix any name, letter, figure, symbol, drawing or any other mark onto a building, road, tree or rock.
  12. Refuse, litter, other objects or harmful liquids may not be thrown at any other person or wild animal.
  13. Visitors may not be in possession of any explosives, unsealed or loaded firearms.
  14. No pets are allowed on Mjejane Game Reserve.
  15. No fires may be lit anywhere other than a place set aside and designated for that purpose.
  16. Visitors may not drive or park in such a manner that it is a nuisance or an inconvenience to any other person or wild animal.
  17. Making noise or doing anything which causes or may cause a hindrance or disturbance to any other person or wild animal is prohibited.
  18. No goods may be advertised for sale on the reserve.
  19. Visitors may not stay overnight at any place other than the Lodge or a place designated for accommodation purposes.
  20. Spotlights may not be used without authorisation and a red filter.
  21. No electronic tracking or telemetry devices may be used, e.g. drones.
  22. Mjejane Game Reserve reserves the right to carry out spot checks of any vehicle entering or exiting the property.
Rules and regulations are enforced under the National Environmental Management: Protected Areas Act, 2003 (Act No. 57 of 2003, revised 2004) and transgressions can result in a fine.
