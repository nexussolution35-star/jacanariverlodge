Types of Mjejane Accommodation

The Mjejane accommodates all tastes and needs.

When selecting your accommodation, you will easily find numerous options to suit your particular needs. From luxury, spacious accommodation to simple camping options, you will be able to find the accommodation that will make your stay in the Mjejane Game Reserve something really special.

Self-catering guesthouses and lodges similar to Jacana are the more popular option, because they tend to have a lot more to offer and are also known for having a more laid-back vibe.

When choosing your accommodation, it is important to choose the type that is compatible with both your needs and your budget. Some accommodation might be cheaper than others, but that could be because it is very basic. Other accommodation could be a little on the pricey side, but in return you will be more than comfortable during your stay.

While the Mjejane Game Reserve continues to see more lodges and guesthouses built, these are currently the three most popular types of accommodation in the reserve.

1. Mjejane Self-Catering Accommodation

Self-catering Mjejane accommodation comes with numerous perks, which is exactly why so many people go for it.

This type of accommodation is often the more affordable kind, because it is up to you to make your own food. Generally, there are no on-site restaurants or cafes, and in some cases there are also no shops nearby.

With this kind of accommodation, guests can come and go as they please, and for the most part they will be left to their own devices, especially as some facilities don’t have staff living on the premises.

Self-catering accommodation in the Mjejane can either be at an actual lodge or guesthouse, or it can take the form of camping accommodation.

Who should choose this type of accommodation?

This accommodation is ideal for families and groups of friends. The extra space, the extra hands to cook and the fact that the overall rate is shared between the group make self-catered lodges the perfect option.

2. Game Lodge

Game lodges are the ideal place to stay if you intend to have a longer stay or if you are travelling from afar.

While game lodges are spacious and have a wide selection of rooms, the rooms often exclude kitchen facilities, as you won’t be preparing your own meals. Game lodges are preferred by those who don’t want the extra worry of packing and preparing their own food.

Private game lodges can have a lot more guests staying on the premises, but that doesn’t mean they won’t deliver on the privacy promise. Game lodges in the Mjejane are quiet spaces, where guests can enjoy disappearing into their own world and simply be on their own, taking in every peaceful moment.

Game lodges are not just great accommodation options; they also often come with a few added extras, like guided game drives and walking tours.

Who should choose this type of Mjejane accommodation?

Anyone who wants an all-inclusive stay should consider a game lodge. Not having to worry about the trip’s finer details, and being able to simply book in and have the rest of the planning taken care of, will allow you more time to relax.

Not having to plan is not for everyone, but it is a big bonus if you are travelling with a limited amount of time.

3. Bed and Breakfast

Bed and breakfast accommodation is not found in abundance among the Mjejane Game Reserve booking options, but there are one or two available for those who want it.

A bed and breakfast is designed simply to provide a comfortable place to sleep and breakfast in the morning.

Bed and breakfast accommodation is usually a short-term option, which is why there are not many of them in the Mjejane.

Who should choose this type of accommodation?

This style of accommodation is the best choice for those looking for a happy middle ground between game lodges and self-catering accommodation. You will still have some control over certain meals of the day, but there is no rush to prepare food in the morning. Bed and breakfasts are also in the middle in terms of pricing: they are neither too expensive nor too cheap.

A Little Bit of History of the Mjejane Game Reserve

The history of the Mjejane Game Reserve is an interesting one. The reserve started out as farmland and, through the vision of those involved, was transformed into one of the premier wildlife reserves in the country, offering safe refuge to millions of wild animals that now roam freely between the Kruger National Park and the Mjejane.

More of that tale from the developers themselves:

“Mjejane Game Reserve is developed by Lugedlane Developments (Pty) Ltd, which is a joint venture between Safrican and the Lugedlane community. In this case Willie and Hennie Joubert, of Safrican, helped successfully restore the land to the Lugedlane Community through the land claims process, covered the shortfall towards the purchase price of the property and ceded personal assets as guarantee in order to obtain the development finance for the servicing of the first phase.

Today this is a glowing example of how, through research, careful planning and selective development, the natural resource was secured for conservation, and has since been successfully maintained and improved. Through the construction, staffing and management, Mjejane is providing a sustainable model for the community members that maximises their income via profits and job creation, and has successfully preserved 4000 hectares of farmland.”

Mjejane Game Reserve on the Map

Straddling 11 km of the lazy brown Crocodile River, Mjejane Game Reserve boasts all the Big 5, a real treat for visitors who are fortunate enough to experience the quiet luxury of the Mjejane away from the bustling Kruger Park.

Mjejane Game Reserve is situated 4 hours’ drive from Johannesburg and 1 hour’s drive from KMIA (Kruger Mpumalanga International Airport). The reserve sits on the Kruger National Park’s southern boundary, right between the Crocodile Bridge Gate and the Malelane Gate of the Kruger National Park, near Hectorspruit (Emjejane).

Frequently Asked Questions about the Mjejane Game Reserve

How does the Mjejane Private Game Reserve compare to other private game reserves?

The Mjejane Private Game Reserve experience far outperforms the standard private game reserve, as all fences have been removed between the Mjejane Private Game Reserve and the Kruger National Park, which is the third-largest game reserve in the world. This allows the free flow of one of the most diverse and most numerous animal populations on earth, which can walk right up to where you and your family are enjoying yourselves in 5-star self-catering luxury at Jacana River Lodge.

Is the Mjejane part of the Kruger?

Not officially, but it shares the Kruger’s wildlife. The Mjejane is a privately owned 4,000-hectare reserve on the Kruger National Park’s southern boundary. The fence between them has been removed, so the Crocodile River is the natural border and the Big 5 roam freely between the two, as part of the Greater Kruger. To drive into the Kruger National Park itself, you leave the Mjejane and enter through a SANParks gate such as Malelane or Crocodile Bridge, where Kruger conservation fees apply.

It is best always to be prepared with your mosquito repellent when heading to the Kruger National Park in the hot summer months, as the park does fall into a malaria area, which includes the Mjejane Game Reserve. What to Expect from a Stay in the Mjejane Game Reserve

Home to the Big 5, with some of the most sought-after views of the most spectacular vistas, and providing guests with a wide range of accommodation options, the Mjejane Game Reserve is a superb destination for anyone wanting to experience the best of what the wild Southern African landscape has to offer. With the Kruger National Park on its doorstep, the Mjejane Game Reserve has everything one could expect from this iconic destination, minus the busy safari company crowds.

Guests visiting the Mjejane Game Reserve experience utter peace and tranquillity. Whether this is your first time visiting the reserve or you are a frequent guest, staying in the Mjejane is a one-of-a-kind experience that will continue to have an impact on you long after you go home.

  1. 01

    1. A Reserve on the Banks of the Crocodile River

    Although there is much to love about this game reserve, and so much to see, one of the greatest pulls of the area is its location. With the Crocodile River running through the reserve, a lot of the best accommodation in the Mjejane has been built to take full advantage of the unique game viewing that can only be experienced along this river’s banks.

    From out-of-this-world, adrenaline-pumping scenes of predators attacking prey, to those pristine sundowner sightings of all sorts of wildlife coming to the river for the last drink of the day, those staying in this reserve can witness it all, enjoying their own sort of private safari without ever having to leave the comfort of their accommodation.

    The Crocodile River is one of the most important rivers in the area. The river starts high up in the Steenkampsberg Mountains of Mpumalanga, close to the misty village of Dullstroom. From its source, the river cascades down towards the Mjejane, flowing, often turbulently, through the Schoemanskloof and Montrose Falls and through the city of Nelspruit, eventually reaching the Mjejane and the Kruger National Park, and finally joining the Komati River at Komatipoort.

    The life supported by the river thrives on its continuously running waters. During a very rainy summer season, the river floods, spilling water and fertile soil across its flood plains. During the winter, the river runs slowly, providing much-needed sustenance to wildlife when the rains are still months away. The river is also home to all kinds of birdlife. Many of the area’s most famous birds build their nests high up in the beautiful indigenous trees that grow close to the river.

    The Crocodile River is also an important part of tourism in the area, especially in places like the Mjejane, where much of the reserve’s accommodation is built to overlook the river and make the most of the incredible views.

    If you want to make the most of the wildlife sightings close to and around the Crocodile River, be sure to check that the accommodation you are considering offers such views. One of the most popular places with a view of the river is Jacana River Lodge, which has been built so that its rooms and its deck give guests the chance to spot all sorts of animals.

  2. 02

    2. What to Expect from a Riverside Holiday in the Mjejane Game Reserve

    Staying along a river’s banks is most appealing to those who want to experience the best possible safari. But there are some things to keep in mind if you want to make your holiday as spectacular as possible.

    1. Be aware of the wildlife. All of the game reserve accommodation in the Mjejane is properly secured, but that doesn’t mean you should be anything less than fully aware of what is happening around the accommodation. Wild animals are for the most part peaceful unless they are disturbed, in which case any animal can become unpredictable. Even if the animals come up close enough to touch, admire them from a distance and always be respectful. Crocodiles and elephants are commonly sighted close to the river banks, and crocodiles in particular are something you should always be on the lookout for when outside.
    2. It might get humid. Having a water source close to your accommodation can make things a lot hotter and more humid. Luckily, most accommodation comes with a swimming pool and/or air conditioning, so your stay should not be too affected by the increased heat.
    3. Be aware of flooding. Although the vast majority of accommodation next to the Crocodile River is built high up, out of reach of the river, when the flooding season comes it is important to be safety conscious, as the fast-moving waters can pose a threat. When the river rises, be sure to stay clear of the water.

    When on holiday in the Mjejane Game Reserve, the river is the source of life and sustenance. It is where you will see some of the best wildlife sightings, and it is the perfect place to sit and look out over on bright mornings and dusky evenings.

Staying in the Mjejane Game Reserve vs. Staying in the Kruger National Park

When deciding whether to stay in the Mjejane or to book a stay in the Kruger National Park, there is much to consider when weighing up your options, but the choice will ultimately come down to personal preference.

Planning a holiday should be based as much as possible on the type of holiday you want to have, and the Mjejane and the Kruger both offer guests a unique experience, each with a lot to look forward to.

  1. 01

    1. The Pros of Staying in the Mjejane Game Reserve

    The location

    Unlike the Kruger National Park, the Mjejane is a lot more exclusive and a lot more private. The reserve doesn’t have day-tourist amenities, and it is not open to day visitors who are just looking to pass through. The reserve is made exclusively for overnight guests, with much of the accommodation tailored to those staying for more than a single night. The reserve’s out-of-reach appeal, and the fact that the roads aren’t busy with day guests, make a holiday here one that is guaranteed to be a lot more laid-back and relaxing.

    The accommodation

    The Mjejane is one of those places one goes to when looking for a more luxurious sort of holiday. While the reserve does have some more rustic accommodation, it is best known for its luxury accommodation, which it has in abundance. The accommodation in the reserve provides everything one could expect from a luxurious stay. From spacious rooms overlooking pristine views of the African landscape to decks kitted out with everything you could need to prepare a fireside meal, accommodation in this reserve has it all.

    The activities

    Unlike the Kruger National Park, the Mjejane doesn’t have as many activities. For the most part, the Kruger has a lot more activities for guests to enjoy, such as movie nights in certain camps and museums to explore. What the reserve does not have is outside safari tour companies coming in with day visitors, something the Kruger has a lot of.

    The game viewing

    By and large, the Mjejane is considered a premier destination for wildlife viewing in Africa, offering a quieter, more private safari than the busier parts of the Kruger National Park. The reserve shares an open, unfenced boundary with the Kruger National Park, so wildlife moves seamlessly between the two. Often more wildlife can be spotted in the Mjejane, especially in those areas where the Crocodile River runs. Guests don’t even have to leave their accommodation to make the most of the sightings, with most of the lodges and guesthouses overlooking the river.

  2. 02

    2. The Pros of Staying in the Kruger National Park

    The location

    The Kruger is a public park, which means it is easily accessible to everyone who wants to visit. This takes away any air of exclusivity, and it also means that the Kruger is a lot busier than the Mjejane. For the most part, the Kruger is really easy to get to, and since it is a huge reserve, there are some places that are quite out of reach and therefore unpopular with the crowds, so if you are looking to escape into the wild, plan accordingly.

    The accommodation

    The huge variety of Kruger Park accommodation allows guests to pick and choose the ideal option for their unique needs and expectations. For those looking for something a little more luxurious, there is higher-end accommodation, and for those wanting something more rustic, there is the option of camping. Each camp in the Kruger has something different, but nothing quite as exclusive and out of the way as the accommodation found in the Mjejane.

    The activities

    Compared with the Mjejane Game Reserve, the Kruger has a whole lot more activities, which can be booked at the camps or included in a pre-packaged Kruger Park safari. Popular activities include game drives, bush walks, social events, visits to camp museums and time spent exploring the many archaeological sites. This can be more appealing to those who want to get out there and mingle with their fellow guests, but for those looking for lazy, relaxed days, the Mjejane can be the best option.

    The game viewing

    The vastness of the Kruger National Park can make game viewing a little more difficult. By contrast, the smaller size of the Mjejane can make it easier to see all of the different types of animals, including the Big 5, without having to spend the whole holiday on the road. Because the Mjejane and the Kruger share an open border, wildlife can move from one area to the next, so the wildlife you can encounter in the Kruger are easily viewed in the Mjejane. And with much of the accommodation built close to the Crocodile River, sometimes guests don’t even have to leave their accommodation to see all sorts of interesting things.

    Both the Kruger and the Mjejane are close to a number of other exciting South African attractions, such as the Panorama Route, the Graskop Gorge Company and numerous interesting small towns all waiting to be explored. When planning your trip, be sure to take these nearby attractions into consideration, as they can add a bit more excitement and variety to your holiday.

    A Mjejane holiday is an unforgettable experience that will stay with you, urging you to make another trip. The weather is perfect all year round, and with such an abundance of wildlife to see, the Mjejane Game Reserve is ideal for that family safari holiday.

    At Jacana River Lodge we have some of the finest accommodation in the reserve, and ours is perfect for long weekend breaks as well as annual family getaways. A large house built to look out over the river, Jacana is a place of peace and quiet, an escape from an otherwise busy world, and the lodge comes with all of the basic amenities to make your stay as comfortable as possible.

    When staying at Jacana, you don’t have to leave the lodge to see the wildlife, with some of the best sightings coming by chance when looking out of a bedroom window or while sitting on the deck enjoying a sundowner.

Mjejane’s Climate Through the Seasons: What to Expect

Situated in the wildest corner of South Africa, the Mjejane is the destination of choice for thousands of adventurers coming to our picturesque country for a safari-style holiday. From the luxury Mjejane accommodation to the many incredible wildlife sightings to be enjoyed, when you make the Mjejane Game Reserve your holiday destination, you can look forward to one of the most memorable and relaxing getaways of your life.

A trip into the Mjejane Game Reserve takes some careful planning. Each month of the year, and each changing season, brings something new and exciting to see and experience. Africa is a continent alive with colours, smells and feelings that you will not find anywhere else on earth. At certain times of the year, things in the Mjejane look and smell different to how you would experience them if you visited at another time. For this reason, and so many others, choosing your season and your month just right will make your trip that little bit more unique to you.

To help you get a better idea of what to expect throughout the year, and to help you pack the right clothing, here’s a look through the seasons in the Mjejane Game Reserve.

Autumn

The light starts to fade at this time of the year, but the sunlight somehow becomes brighter. As the last of the rains fall and the heat quickly begins to retreat, a rich, dry, earthy smell fills the air. Autumn is considered one of the best times of the year to visit, simply because it is not so unbearably hot. And as the grasses begin to die back, and animals are no longer sheltering from the heat, wildlife sightings at all times of the day become the norm once more.

Autumns are a special time. While the Mjejane Game Reserve has a few evergreens, there are also deciduous trees and shrubs whose colours begin to change to soft yellows, bright oranges and finally deep reds, before shedding, only to return in the spring.

At this time of the year, you can enjoy delightfully warm days and evenings spent around a fire, bundled up and enjoying the busy sounds of bats and birds.

Winter

The days are mild, peaking at around 26 degrees Celsius on a good day and 14 degrees on a cold one. The earth is dry again, so dry that there is barely a green stem or leaf in sight. But nature finds a way to carry on, and wildlife can be seen in great numbers close to watering holes and near the plants and trees that do not lose their greenery.

The days might be shorter, but this is certainly considered by many to be the very best time of the year to visit the Mjejane Game Reserve. There is just so much to see, and if you are visiting from a colder climate, you will find the winter days a lot more comfortable than the intense summer heat.

The lack of dense bush makes wildlife sightings an incredibly common occurrence, so if you have a special bucket list of wildlife that you hope to see, this is certainly the right time to book your trip. Depending on where you are staying, you can expect to see the animals right up close to your accommodation.

It barely ever rains here at this time of year, and there is certainly no chance of snow, but you should bring along extra moisturiser, because when the wind starts to kick up, as it does in our part of the world, it can be harsh on your skin. You should also bring along sunblock, because our winters are always sunny.

And if you are travelling late in the season, keep in mind that in the Mjejane Game Reserve winter can turn into spring overnight, so packing some lighter clothing is a must when travelling in August and September.

Spring

The buds are forming and some are blooming, filling the air with a most heady fragrance; the gentle first rains have dampened the parched earth; and the new babies have been born.

Spring is one of those times of the year that is magical no matter where you are in the world, but in the Mjejane it is extra special, as the energy and excitement of the coming warmer months can be felt everywhere, from the park staff and visitors to the wildlife.

Although spring in the northern hemisphere can still be quite cold, with light frosts, in Africa spring can feel like the middle of summer on some days. The heat comes quite quickly, and with it come brighter early mornings and a later sunset, giving you more time to enjoy the great outdoors and the many animal sightings the Mjejane has to offer. But while the days might be warmer, bringing along some warmer clothing is a must to fend off the sudden end-of-winter cool spells that sometimes return unexpectedly.

Because the intense summer rains and storms have not yet arrived, the bushveld is still quite dry, which is a plus for you, because the dry vegetation makes it so much easier to spot wildlife. Once the rains arrive, the bush becomes dense and overgrown, making it that little bit harder to see animals.

Summer

Summers in the Mjejane include something that you won’t be able to experience at any other time of the year, and that is thunderstorms. Nothing is as magnificent as an African thunderstorm. The energy in the air as majestic storm clouds begin to gather in the late morning, and the lightning begins its dance in the distance, is palpable.

And with the rains come a lot more humidity and the thickening of grass and bush.

Since the days get so warm, safari drives in the Mjejane Game Reserve are best reserved for early mornings and late afternoons, when animals are not hiding from the heat.

Regarded by many as one of the less favourable times of the year to visit the Mjejane, summer can actually be a wonderful season to holiday in the reserve if you are not too concerned about working up a sweat.

You already know that summers in Africa are on a planet of their own. The days not only regularly reach highs of 34 to 42 degrees Celsius, but are also often blanketed in 80 to 100% humidity. With good planning, and by making sure that your accommodation offers air conditioning and a swimming pool, you’ll be glad you decided to have a taste of a truly African summer.

Wildlife

The Big 5 of the Mjejane

Situated on the southern boundary of the Kruger, the Mjejane lets you see all of the Big 5, perhaps even from the comfort of the lodge.

Before the fences between the Kruger and the reserves around it came down, wildlife were stopped by fences which changed their natural migratory patterns. With the fences down, wildlife can once more move freely according to their ancient patterns, giving guests a unique view of how the wildlife move.

  1. 01

    1. Rhino

    With so many hunters once flocking to Africa, the white rhino population in the Lowveld was hunted to extinction. In 1961, 337 rhinos were reintroduced to the area, and the populations have since flourished. The rhino of Mjejane have crossed the river to make a home in the reserve, which is also home to a few shy black rhino.

  2. 02

    2. Elephant

    Some guests have spotted elephants from the lodge swimming pool, while others see them out on safari. The elephants of the Mjejane live in matriarchal societies and are often spotted at the Crocodile River, where they can each drink up to 17 litres of water per visit.

  3. 03

    3. Buffalo

    Like other grazing animals, the Mjejane buffalo herds crossed into the reserve in search of food and territory. They keep the grasslands maintained, which opens the area to smaller antelope. Guests often see the lone older bull living a bachelor lifestyle, best enjoyed from a safe distance.

  4. 04

    4. Lion

    The mighty kings and queens of the Kruger savannah have made their way into Mjejane, lured by the scent of prey. Working in prides, they are often spotted taking down prey along the Crocodile River, and occasionally with their cubs, which generally appear between February and April.

  5. 05

    5. Leopard

    The leopard tops many a wildlife list, yet remains elusive. With the thick riverine forest (a natural leopard habitat) and a reserve much smaller than the Kruger, guests at Mjejane have a better opportunity to spot these quiet big cats, especially close to the Crocodile River.
