# Between Game Drives: A Family Day Beside the Crocodile River

A safari holiday has more hours than the ones spent on a game vehicle. There is the first cup of coffee, the conversation after breakfast and that quiet stretch when nobody needs to be anywhere. At [Jacana River Lodge in Mjejane Game Reserve](../), the Crocodile River gives those hours a setting of their own. For families choosing accommodation near Kruger National Park, that is worth considering: what will your holiday feel like between the outings?

Think of the following as a possible day, rather than a timetable. Your family can adapt it around booked activities, children's energy and the pleasure of staying somewhere you want to spend time.

## Start with a view instead of a to-do list

Before breakfast becomes a group discussion, take your coffee outside and settle into the morning. Leave the phone within reach for a photograph, but give yourself a few minutes before opening messages. Look across the river, then look again. The interest of a natural landscape often emerges slowly: a movement, a change in light, a bird you initially overlooked.

Bring binoculars if you have them. Let children take turns choosing a patch of the landscape to watch. There does not need to be a spectacular sighting for the exercise to work. Learning to pay attention is part of the pleasure, and wildlife encounters remain unpredictable.

## Make breakfast part of being together

A self-catering safari gives breakfast room to become whatever the morning needs. On a drive day, that might mean something simple before departure. On a lodge morning, it could mean everyone drifting towards the table at a gentler pace.

Keep the preparation easy. Ask one person to handle drinks, another to put out food and someone else to clear the table afterwards. A shared holiday works better when the work is shared too. Sitting down together is the point; producing an elaborate breakfast for an audience is optional.

## Give the river your unhurried attention

Accommodation beside the Crocodile River offers a reason to stay put for a while. You can return to the same outlook several times and notice something different, even when the scene initially appears still. Try comparing what you see before breakfast with what catches your attention later.

For children, turn observation into a small challenge. Find three different sounds. Describe a bird without naming it. Sketch the shape of the opposite bank. These activities need no special equipment, and they give younger travellers something to do beyond waiting for adults to announce an animal.

## Choose a comfortable afternoon pace

At some point, the family will want different things. Someone wants a swim, someone wants a book and someone wants a nap. That is a perfectly good afternoon. Jacana's veranda, deck and rim-flow pool make the outdoor spaces part of the stay, while the bedrooms offer somewhere to retreat.

The property has five en-suite bedrooms for up to ten guests. Bedrooms include air-conditioning and ceiling fans, giving everyone somewhere comfortable to relax during time at the lodge. Shared river-facing areas mean enjoying the outlook does not have to depend on which bedroom you occupy.

## Collect a few photographs with a story

A memorable holiday album can show more than animal close-ups. Photograph the breakfast table before everyone sits down, a pair of binoculars beside a book or the family gathered for lunch. Ask before sharing identifiable photographs of other guests, especially children.

Try a simple three-picture story: morning, afternoon and evening from your stay. Keep the captions specific to what actually happened. A small, honest detail often carries more personality than another grand claim about a perfect escape. Save the editing for later so taking photographs does not swallow the experience.

## Let a game drive provide a different perspective

Time at the lodge and time exploring Mjejane complement each other. A drive takes you beyond the view you have been watching, adding different landscapes and opportunities to observe wildlife. Afterwards, familiar surroundings can prompt new questions about what you noticed while you were out.

Check the lodge's [current game-drive arrangements](../facilities/) before travelling. Plan meals around the confirmed departure rather than assuming activities will fit around lunch. Leave a comfortable gap afterwards as well: children may be hungry, and the adults may simply want to talk about the outing.

## Make dinner the day's meeting point

When everyone comes back together, keep dinner straightforward. A meal you know how to prepare leaves more attention for the people around you. Decide responsibilities before lighting the braai, including who will handle the less glamorous clearing away.

Invite each person to name one thing they noticed that day. The answers need not involve wildlife. A child might remember winning a game; a grandparent might choose the quiet morning. Those differences tell you something useful about what makes a holiday enjoyable for your particular family.

## Questions families ask about staying beside the river

### Can you enjoy a safari holiday without filling every day with drives?

Yes. You can combine booked drives with time enjoying the lodge and observing the surrounding landscape. Choose the balance your group will enjoy, while remembering that wildlife sightings from any location are never guaranteed.

### Is a river view the same as river access?

No. Enjoy the view from the lodge's designated guest areas and follow the property's safety instructions. A river-facing holiday does not imply permission to walk to the water or approach wildlife.

### What should we bring for quieter lodge time?

Binoculars, a book, a sketchpad and a favourite family game are useful starting points. Choose a few things your group already enjoys instead of packing activities for every possible spare minute.
