### A Word about the Mjejane

Africans hold a special place in their hearts for the wilderness and the wildlife that characterise our home. We all feel a special connection to this place and a definite desire to preserve every part of it. This wide stretch of land was set aside by Chief Lugedlane with the intention of preserving the land and its wildlife for future generations to enjoy. His vision was to create an inheritance in a way that is so distinctly African. Our inheritance is the wildlife that we are here to look after and it is because of the visions of people like Chief Lugedlane that Africa’s wildlife lives on, untouched, in the very habitat that once belonged to their ancestors. The land still belongs to the Mjejane community, and those who have built their self-catering game lodges here understand the importance of honouring the commitment to preserving the land.

  

### Wildlife of the Mjejane Private Nature Reserve

Since the Mjejane Private Nature Reserve is situated on the Southern Boundary of the Kruger National Park, those who stay here can spot just about every animal the area has to offer. The Southern Kruger is famous for its rich wildlife population and guests can see all of the Big 5 as well as all sorts of other interesting animals. Wildlife migrate throughout the year. Before the areas around the Kruger became a part of the park, wildlife were stopped by the fences which meant that their natural migratory patterns were completely changed. In some cases, this halt resulted in wildlife having to change feeding habits as well as certain other natural instincts that once filled with the urge to make the move. With the fences coming down, and more land being incorporated into the Kruger area, wildlife could once more move freely, according to their ancient patterns. This movement is equally beneficial for those visiting the Mjejane, as it gives guests a unique view of how the wildlife move. The animals that you will see while staying at Mjejane Private Nature Reserve are not just those that migrate. The reserve is full of all sorts of wildlife as well as a colourful array of some of the most spectacular birds. Given the size of the nature reserve, the animals you can see while visiting the Mjejane can include just about every type that this part of the world is well known for.

  

### The Big 5 of the Mjejane

The legendary status of the Big 5 means they appear on just about every safari bucket list, and when you visit the Mjejane, you can see them all, perhaps even from the comfort of our self-catering game lodge in Mpumalanga.
The Big 5 are a protected group of animals, which makes their naming quite ironic. These famous animals were given their name by hunters who would come to Africa, mostly from Europe, to make a trophy of these majestic creatures.
The hunters would traverse the rough African terrain on foot, with their simple rifles in hand. Often, they would become the hunted as the Big 5 were at that time considered the most dangerous animals to hunt.
The danger factor is what earned the animals a place in the Big 5. If a hunter could kill one of them, they were indeed talented hunters.

* Rhino

With so many hunters flocking to Africa, it didn’t take long for the white rhino population in the Lowveld to be hunted to extinction. In 1961, 337 rhinos were reintroduced to the area and since then the populations have more or less flourished, although the ever present danger of poachers has resulted in the Kruger and surrounding nature reserves having to improve their security efforts.
The rhino of Mjejane have crossed the river to make a home for themselves in the reserve. The reserve is also home to a few black rhino, although they are rather shy.

  

* Elephant

Some guests staying at Jacana River Lodge have had the luck of spotting elephants from the lodge swimming pool, while others will normally see elephants while out on a safari. Before the Mjejane was a part of the Kruger National Park, it was primarily used as agricultural land, which means the elephant herds not only have a reliable food source, but they also have a large area of land to spread out across. When the elephants moved in, the land took a bit of a beating, with trees and other vegetation destroyed, but the land has since recovered. The elephants of the Mjejane live in matriarchal societies and the herds are often spotted at the Crocodile River, where they can each drink up to 17 litres of water upon each visit.

  

* Buffalo

Like the other grazing animals seeking greener pastures, the Mjejane buffalo herds crossed into the game reserve in search of food and territory. They have proven to be a valuable asset to the conservation area as they keep the grasslands maintained which in turn opens the area to smaller antelope.
The buffalos live in large herds, but guests do often see the lone buffalo, generally an older male now separated from the herd and living a bachelor lifestyle. Buffalo have always had a bit of a reputation for being aggressive, especially when feeling threatened, and guests are encouraged to enjoy any buffalo sighting from a safe distance.

  

* Lion

The mighty Kings and Queens of the Kruger savannah have also made their way into Mjejane, lured by the appealing scent of prey.
Working in prides, the lions are often spotted taking down prey along the Crocodile River, and they are also occasionally spotted with their cubs, which generally appear between February and April each year. Spotting a lion while in the Mjejane is an unbelievable experience as they can come quite close to the lodge, giving guests a once in a lifetime sighting.

  

* Leopard

The leopard is at the top of many a wildlife sighting list, and yet for many guests it remains an elusive creature.
With the thick riverine forest, a natural habitat for leopards, and the fact that the reserve is a lot smaller than the Kruger, guests visiting the Mjejane have a better opportunity to spot these quiet big cats.
The best way to see a leopard is by spending some time close to the Crocodile River, where leopard populations are impressively big.
